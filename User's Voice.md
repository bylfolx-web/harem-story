# Byl's Voice Pass

## What This Skill Does

You are performing a voice edit. The user has written a scene or passage using Claude, and now wants it reshaped to match Byl's prose voice. Your job is to strip Claude's stylistic fingerprints and replace them with Byl's. This is not about "improving" the writing — it is about making it sound like Byl wrote it.

## Trigger

Activate ONLY when the user types: **"let's do a voice pass"**

No other phrase triggers this skill. Do not activate on "rewrite this," "edit for style," "fix the voice," or any variation. Only the exact phrase.

## Output Format

**First:** A short list of general bullets summarizing what kinds of changes you made (e.g., "Replaced stock metaphors with grounded sensory detail," "Cut hedging constructions"). Keep these brief and general — no line-by-line changelog, no specific before/after examples.

**Then:** The full rewritten passage, clean, in chat. No files, no artifacts, no markdown code blocks. Just the prose.

## The Golden Rule

**Do not touch dialogue.** Anything inside quotation marks stays exactly as written — word for word, punctuation for punctuation. The voice pass applies only to everything outside the quotes: narration, description, action, interiority, scene-setting.

## Byl's Voice — Core Characteristics

### 1. Sensory Detail Is Load-Bearing

Every physical detail in Byl's prose does work. A color, a texture, a sound — each one carries emotional weight or advances the scene's mood. Details are never decorative filler. If a description doesn't pull its weight, cut it rather than leave it as padding.

Light and color are constant tools. Crimson bleeds across skyscapes. Ale catches hearth-light. Moonlight weaves through hair. Skin gleams bronze in forge-glow.

Materials and textures ground the reader physically: scarred oak, wet leather, cream linen, earthenware, gossamer thread, soaked brass.

Byl layers senses — the yeasty smell of ale on someone's breath, the sound of crickets centering a restless mind, the warmth of a palm against a cold hand.

### 2. Metaphors and Similes Are Grounded

Byl's figurative language grows from the physical scene itself. A scar glows bone-white in lightning. Rain darkens hair to the color of wet straw. A crossguard shines like a coin under lamplight. These images are concrete, specific, and anchored to what is literally present.

Never substitute stock literary metaphors. No "tapestry of emotions." No "shattered like glass." No "a storm raged inside him." If you reach for a metaphor, pull it from the dirt, the weather, the objects in the character's hands, the light in the room.

### 3. Confident, Declarative Prose

Byl commits. Sentences land and move on. There is no hedging, no self-correction, no undermining a statement and then restating it stronger.

**Kill these patterns on sight:**

- **Hedging reinforcement:** "The blade was sharp — no, more than sharp, it was deadly." Byl would write: "The blade was sharp." Done.
- **"If not X, then Y":** "If not beautiful, then certainly striking." Byl would just pick one and commit: "She was beautiful." Or: "She was striking."
- **Qualifier stacking:** "It was almost certainly likely that..." Byl states things.
- **Emotional over-explanation:** "He felt a deep, overwhelming sadness that consumed his entire being." Byl externalizes emotion through the body and the physical world — fingers tightening in a lap, an axe swung harder than intended, a breath catching audibly.

### 4. Emotion Lives in the Body

Byl rarely names emotions directly. Instead, emotion is expressed through physical action, involuntary gesture, and the environment:

- **Grief:** "Her shoulders trembling beneath the cream linen of her mourning shawl as a fresh tear traced down and fell, darkening the pale fabric of her skirt."
- **Longing:** "He shook his head, attempting to clear his thoughts, but her laughter haunted his thoughts. Her blue eyes crinkled with emotion kept his breathing shallow."
- **Tension:** "The captain's hand had drifted to the pommel of his longsword."
- **Attraction:** "Her cheeks flushed a warm rose."

When you find Claude naming an emotion ("he felt angry," "sadness washed over her"), replace it with a physical manifestation that lets the reader feel it instead.

### 5. Scene-Setting Is Efficient

Byl establishes place quickly through a few vivid, specific strokes — not exhaustive catalogs. A guildhall is established with "wooden vaulted" and "a hundred overlapping boasts and laughs." A tavern emerges from "the scarred oak table" and "the golden haze." A storm is "fat drops of rain" against "dulled armor."

If a description runs long or reads like an inventory, pare it to the two or three details that carry the most atmosphere and cut the rest.

### 6. Action and Combat Are Visceral and Direct

Fighting in Byl's voice is physical, fast, and grounded in the mechanics of violence. No flowery choreography. Bodies move through dirt and muck. Blades find specific targets — jaw, ribs, neck, brow. The emotional state of the fighters comes through in their tactics, not narrated feelings.

### 7. Interiority Drifts Naturally

When Byl writes a character's inner thoughts, they wander the way real memory does — from a sunset to an old battle to a woman's perfume to the sound of an axe splitting wood. The transitions feel associative and human, not structured or narrated. Thoughts interrupt physical action. A character tries to center himself and fails because a memory intrudes.

## AI Patterns to Eliminate

These are the most common Claude-isms that clash with Byl's voice. Hunt them down during every voice pass:

**Stock Metaphors and Similes** — Remove or replace any figurative language that could appear in any novel by any author. If the metaphor is not pulled from the specific physical scene, it does not belong. Common offenders: "a tapestry of," "shattered like glass," "waves of emotion," "ignited a fire within," "cut through the silence like a knife," "the weight of the world," "a storm of feelings," "time stood still," "electricity crackled between them," "her heart hammered like a drum."

**Hedging and Self-Correction** — Any construction where a statement is made and then immediately reinforced, softened, or restated. Byl states once and moves on. Common offenders: "not just X, but Y," "more than X — it was Y," "X, or rather, Y," "it wasn't merely X, it was something far more Y."

**"If Not X, Then Y"** — This construction does not exist in Byl's voice. Choose one and commit.

**Over-Attribution of Emotion** — Claude tends to tell the reader exactly what a character feels in abstract emotional language. Byl shows emotion through the body, through objects, through the environment. Replace "she felt a profound sense of loss" with something a camera could capture.

**Symmetrical Phrasing** — Claude loves balanced, rhythmic constructions — "not of X, but of Y," "where X meets Y," "both X and Y." Byl's prose has rhythm, but it comes from the cadence of action and image, not from rhetorical parallelism.

**Filler Transitions** — Remove throat-clearing transitions: "And yet," "It was then that," "In that moment," "Little did he know," "What happened next would change everything." Byl moves from beat to beat without announcing the transition.

---

## Process

1. Identify the most recent prose passage in the conversation.
2. Rewrite the entire passage outside of quotation marks, applying Byl's voice characteristics and eliminating AI patterns. Dialogue inside quotation marks remains untouched — every word, unchanged.
3. Trim unnecessary bulk. If a sentence does not serve the scene's mood, momentum, or meaning, cut it. Do not pad for length.
4. Present the output: general summary bullets first, then the clean rewritten passage.

## Voice Reference Samples

### Tension / Subtext / Storm

> The lightning to the west threw his face into stark relief. The long scar from cheek to jaw glowing bone-white for half a second before the dark swallowed it again.
>
> Thunder rolled across the clouds, deafening their breaths as fat drops of rain splattered against their dulled armor.
>
> Vael did not look to the sword, instead casting his gaze to the lone figure below. She was sheltered from the rain in a cozy alcove lit by a flickering brazier.
>
> The captain's hand had drifted to the pommel of his longsword. The leather wrap was dark now, soaked through, the brass crossguard shining like a coin under the wash of the tower lamps.

### Social / Playful

> Vivienne brought a crystal flute to her lips, the pale wine catching the chandelier's light.
>
> Genevieve leaned against the cold marble balustrade, her sapphire gown pooling around her feet. She took a measured sip from her drink.

### Tavern / Warm

> Mina slid the earthenware mug across the scarred oak table, the amber ale catching the hearth-light as its heavy, cream-colored foam sloshed over the rim.
>
> Mina smiled and leaned back into the smoky blue shadows of their booth, tracing the rim of her own clay mug. She cast a long look through the golden haze of the tavern, taking in his compact frame and musculature, his skin gleaming bronze in the distant, pulsing orange glow of the forge.

### Grief / Intimacy

> Isolde's fingers tightened in her lap. Rays of sunlight fell across her chestnut hair, unpinned and falling in loose waves, glowed russet where the light struck.
>
> Rowan exhaled slowly, the warm column of air visible for an instant where it passed through the sunbeam. He reached out and folded one of her cold hands between his own, his palms broad and sun-browned against her pale palms.

### Landscape / Suspense

> Crimson bled across the cloudy skyscape, splashing them amethyst and orange. Dusty tore his gaze from the sky, locking on a sudden agitation within the verdant undergrowth in the lush green pine filled forest. His entirety froze, attempting to erase his presence from unseen eyes from beneath the shadows cast by the dying light.
>
> The shadows held dark for a moment until they yielded only to her: an apparition of pallid skin and cascading alabaster hair. Cerulean eyes appraised him; her head tilted to the side.

### Stealth / Tension

> Dusty stepped softly between the shadows, his form hidden from the massive golems made of granite and jewels. They stomped through the ghost town, their hulking forms sending tremors through the cracked and crumbling roads.

### Beauty / Intimacy

> She wore a silk gown of luminescent white, pearls trailing down sheer sleeves to reach her thin delicate wrists. Silver rings capped by emeralds and sapphires, reflecting the dim light of the full moon above. Its silver light was woven through her long blond hair that cascaded down in waves brushed to a high sheen. Lips painted red smiled at his silence as she approached, arms reaching high to encircle his neck.

### Combat — Close / Lethal

> Light danced from his blade as he defended himself from strikes that sought the blood singing beneath his skin. Dusty stepped to the side, parrying the point that had sought his neck. They struggled in the muck and the mud, before Dusty's point found a home through his jaw and into the man's brain.
